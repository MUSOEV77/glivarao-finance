## 7. How scenes illustrate the narration

Clearly visible even in thumbnails: **every idea becomes a literal visual metaphor.** "Hiding the truth"
becomes a character hiding behind a newspaper. "AI crash" becomes an angry robot. "France" becomes a
worried France map, and contagion becomes a crowd of small worried countries.

Our sample illustrates the *topic* of a scene (piggy bank, target). It does not illustrate *each
sentence*. Max gestures in front of a room for about 10 seconds per scene. **This is the single biggest
quality gap.** The fix is in the script as much as the renderer: every sentence needs a planned visual
"beat".

## 8. Animation, transitions and pacing _[genre, unverified]_

Conventions of this genre:
- A new visual beat every **3–6 seconds**, matching the narration.
- Lines that **draw on** stroke by stroke, then hold with a slight wobble.
- One continuous white canvas the camera **glides across**, instead of cutting between rooms.
- Charts drawn left to right in sync with the voice, with the endpoint highlighted when the number is spoken.
- Simple cuts or pans, and very few flashy transitions.

Ours: about 10 seconds per scene, a set change with iris or push transitions every scene, and a
character who talks but rarely changes what the viewer understands.

## 9. Voice, sound design and delivery _[genre, unverified]_

Genre norm:
- One calm, confident male narrator, measured and slightly ominous, at about 150–165 words per minute.
- Conversational asides; the narration often addresses the viewer directly.
- A quiet music bed throughout, with stings at reveals.
- Light sound effects: pencil scratches on draw-ons, whooshes on camera moves, paper and cash sounds.

Ours: Kokoro am_michael, clear and natural but neutral, with limited dramatic range. There is **no music
and no sound effects at all**, which makes the sample feel empty compared with any professional channel.

## 10. Titles, thumbnails and structure

**Titles (from the screenshot).** These are long macro-economic claims with tension words: "Smart People
… Looking for the Exit", "Nobody Wants To Admit…", "Next Great Crisis", "This Time…", "Choking the
Economy". They are about **systemic events** (AI bubble, real estate, sovereign debt, margin debt, cost of
money), they are timely (2026), and the title and thumbnail **complement** each other rather than repeat.

**Ours.** Evergreen personal-finance lessons ("compound interest, explained"). **This positioning is kept:
the channel stays finance-only.** What we take from the reference is *packaging craft*, not topics:
- a short emotional serif reaction line on the thumbnail;
- a title that adds tension or curiosity instead of repeating the thumbnail;
- a real number in the hook.

Applied to finance-only subjects, for example: "The Minimum Payment Trap Costs More Than You Think", or
the thumbnail line "It Never Ends.". The rule is tension, not fear, and every claim must be sourced.

**Structure _[genre, unverified]_.** A cold-open hook with the most striking real number in the first
20–30 seconds, then:
1. why it matters now;
2. a relatable money story or historical parallel (for GLIVARAO: a person's real-life money decision, never politics);
3. the mechanism;
4. the evidence, chart after chart;
5. the counter-argument;
6. what it means for ordinary people;
7. a closing thought.

### Where the art comes from (the main quality question)

A 16-minute video at one beat every 4 seconds needs **about 240 illustrated beats**. Options:
1. **Code-drawn library (free, what we do now).** Fully original and consistent, but limited variety. Each new prop takes development time.
2. **Open-licensed icon sets restyled as sketches (free).** For example MIT-licensed SVG icon sets, converted to wobbly ink lines. Fast variety for objects. Check each license.
3. **Commissioned illustrator (paid, best result).** A character model sheet plus about 300 props and metaphors, which the engine then poses and animates. Most consistent and most "real hand-drawn".
4. **AI image generation in a fixed doodle style (paid, needs a service).** Fast variety, but character consistency and quality control take work.

**Recommendation:** start with options 1 and 2 to prove the pipeline. Then budget for option 3 once the
style is approved. That is the step that gets closest to "real hand-drawn" quality.
